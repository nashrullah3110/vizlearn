Information Gain

Information gain measures how much a split reduces uncertainty about a target variable.

The high-cardinality trap

Information gain can favour features that create many small groups, including identifiers. A tree may appear to fit training data better while generalising worse. Validation, regularisation, and alternative criteria such as gain ratio can address this, depending on the task. Entropy itself is defined in entropy and information; the decision-tree lesson applies the split criterion.

Overview

Entropy before and after a split

For target labels Y, entropy is H(Y) = −Σᵧ p(y)log₂p(y). A feature split divides the data into groups. Information gain is the original entropy minus the weighted entropy remaining in those groups:

IG = H(Y) − Σₖ P(Gₖ)H(Y|Gₖ)

Suppose a balanced binary target has entropy 1 bit. A split that creates two pure groups has conditional entropy 0 and information gain 1 bit. A split that leaves both groups equally mixed gives no gain. Weighting by group size matters: a tiny pure group should not count as much as a large one.
