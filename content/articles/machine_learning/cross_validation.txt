title: K-Fold Cross-Validation
intro: K-fold cross-validation evaluates a model on several held-out parts of a dataset. It shows how the score varies across splits and supports model comparison when the split matches the data.

## How the folds work

Divide the examples into (k) folds. Fit (k) models: each run trains on (k-1) folds and evaluates on the remaining fold. Every row is held out once. With 100 rows and (k=5), each run trains on 80 rows and evaluates on 20.

Average the fold scores for a summary and inspect their spread. The standard deviation describes variation across these folds; it is not a confidence interval, because fold scores are not independent samples. Cross-validation estimates how the training procedure performs on similar data, not a guarantee for future populations.

## Follow one evaluation

Set **Folds (k)** to 5 and press **Run Next Fold** until each fold has been held out. The visualizer shows which block is used for training and which for evaluation. Press **Auto-Run All** to compare the scores. A low-scoring fold may reveal a subgroup or an unstable split; do not hide it by reporting only the mean.

## Choosing a split strategy

Five or ten folds are common practical choices, but the best value depends on data size, computation, and the task. Larger (k) means each model trains on a larger share of the data but requires more fits. Very large (k), including leave-one-out, can be computationally expensive and does not automatically produce a more reliable estimate.

- **Classification:** stratified folds can preserve class proportions when random splitting is appropriate.
- **Repeated people, devices, or documents:** use group-aware folds so one entity cannot occur on both sides.
- **Time series:** use forward-chaining or time-series splits so training precedes evaluation.

Random k-fold is not suitable for every dataset. A split that leaks groups or future information gives a precise-looking answer to the wrong question.

## Model selection and the final test

If you use the same cross-validation scores to choose among many models or settings, the best score tends to be optimistic because selection favored a lucky result. Use nested cross-validation when you need an estimate of the full tuning process, or keep an untouched test set for a final evaluation after choices are complete. Fit preprocessing separately inside each training fold, typically with a pipeline.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating fold standard deviation as a confidence interval:** the folds share much of their training data.
- **Using random folds for temporal or grouped data:** the split must reflect how new examples arrive.
- **Tuning on the final test set:** repeated choices turn it into validation data.
- **Comparing different folds for competing models:** use the same splits to make comparisons more informative.
