title: Outliers and Influence
intro: An outlier has an unusual value; an influential observation changes a fitted model substantially. Check both ideas before deciding whether to correct or exclude a point.

## Three related ideas

A **large residual** means an outcome is far from the model’s prediction; **high leverage** means its predictors are unusual. A point is **influential** if removing it changes the fitted model. These are different properties: an unusual outcome may have little influence, while a high-leverage point can still fit the trend.

In least-squares regression, residuals are squared. A point twice as far from the fitted line contributes four times as much squared error. This makes a distant observation capable of pulling the line toward itself.

## Explore the fitted line

In the visualizer, move the point vertically while keeping its x-position near the middle, then move it to the edge of the x-range. A point near the mean x has less leverage on the slope; an extreme x-value can rotate the line more. If that edge point also sits far from the trend, it can combine high leverage and a large residual, making it influential.

Cook’s distance combines residual size and leverage to estimate how much a fitted regression would change if an observation were removed. It is a diagnostic, not an automatic deletion rule; no single cutoff decides whether a row is an error or should be excluded.

## Decide what the observation means

1. **Data error:** check units, transcription, sensor failures, and sentinel values such as 999. Correct or remove errors and document the choice.
2. **Different population:** a wholesale order may not belong in a model for ordinary retail purchases. Decide whether it is outside the question or deserves a separate model.
3. **Valid extreme case:** keep it if the model is meant to cover such cases. Compare results with and without it and report sensitivity when conclusions change.

Deleting points simply because they make a fit less convenient changes the population being modeled. Investigate first; if the cause is uncertain, show how the conclusion depends on the point.

## More robust alternatives

Huber regression uses squared loss for small residuals and a loss that grows more slowly for large residuals. RANSAC fits candidate models on subsets and selects a consensus set. These methods can reduce sensitivity to some outliers, but they rely on assumptions too and may ignore valid rare cases. Transforming a strongly skewed feature can also help when the scale is the issue.

## Common mistakes

- **Deleting every point beyond three standard deviations:** the cutoff assumes a distribution and the point can inflate the standard deviation.
- **Confusing unusual x with unusual y:** leverage and residuals measure different properties.
- **Treating robust regression as automatic cleanup:** inspect which observations receive less influence and why.
- **Ignoring data quality:** an impossible value is a data problem before it is a modeling problem.
