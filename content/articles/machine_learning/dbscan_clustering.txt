title: DBSCAN Clustering
intro: DBSCAN groups points connected through dense neighbourhoods and marks isolated points as noise. It can find irregular shapes without choosing the number of clusters first.

## Core, border, and noise points

DBSCAN groups points with dense neighbourhoods instead of requiring a preset cluster count. **eps** sets the neighbourhood radius and **minPts** sets how many points must be nearby, including the point itself; sparse points can remain noise.

- A **core point** has at least `minPts` points in its `eps` neighbourhood.
- A **border point** is within `eps` of a core point but has too few neighbours to be core.
- A **noise point** is neither core nor reachable from one.

Starting from a core point, DBSCAN expands through neighbouring core points and includes their border points. A chain of connected dense regions can trace a curved cluster; no distance to a cluster centre is used.

## Explore the parameters

Choose **Data** as **Two crescents**, then adjust **eps (neighbourhood radius)** and **minPts**. If `eps` is too small, points become noise or clusters fragment. If it is too large, separate groups can merge. Raising `minPts` requires stronger local support, which often labels more points as noise. Switch **Data** to **Three blobs and some noise** and compare how points are assigned.

These settings depend on feature scale and distance metric. Standardize features when their units or ranges differ substantially; otherwise a large-scale feature can dominate the neighbourhood distance. A sorted k-distance plot can help identify a candidate `eps`, but its knee is a heuristic that still needs validation.

## When DBSCAN is a good fit

It is useful when clusters may be non-convex, the number of groups is unknown, and outliers should not be forced into a cluster. K-means is often simpler and faster for compact, similarly scaled groups when every point needs an assignment.

DBSCAN struggles when groups have very different densities because one radius may be too small for a sparse group and too large for a dense one. High-dimensional distances can also become less informative. HDBSCAN explores density structure across scales and may help, but it brings its own choices and is not a universal fix.

## Common mistakes

- **Using unscaled features with incompatible units:** the radius then reflects the largest-scale feature.
- **Treating noise as an error:** noise is a legitimate output; inspect it before deciding what it means.
- **Assuming the number of groups is always recovered:** parameter settings can merge, split, or erase clusters.
- **Using DBSCAN as a predictor for new points without checking support:** the original method labels a fitted dataset; new-point assignment needs a defined procedure.
