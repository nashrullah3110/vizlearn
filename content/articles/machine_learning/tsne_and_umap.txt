title: t-SNE and UMAP beside PCA
intro: PCA, t-SNE, and UMAP project high-dimensional data into a plot for different purposes. PCA captures directions of variance; t-SNE and UMAP focus more on preserving local neighbourhoods.

## Three different projections

**PCA** is a linear projection onto directions with high variance. A two-dimensional PCA plot shows how much of the original variation those two components retain, but it can hide structure in omitted dimensions. PCA does not preserve every pairwise distance when reducing dimensions.

**t-SNE** constructs a layout that tries to keep nearby points together. **UMAP** builds a neighbourhood graph and optimizes a low-dimensional representation of it. Both are nonlinear and can produce useful views of local structure. Neither plot is a faithful map of all high-dimensional distances.

## Compare the layouts

The visualization starts from groups with known spacing and compares how each method lays them out. Switch between PCA and the neighbourhood methods, then change the local-neighbourhood setting. The groups may remain visibly separated even as their displayed gaps change. This shows why cluster separation in the picture is useful for exploration but not proof that the groups are globally far apart in the original data.

## What the plot can and cannot tell you

- **Nearby points:** a t-SNE or UMAP plot is designed to emphasize local neighbourhoods, but validate the neighbourhoods in the original feature space.
- **Gaps between groups:** do not interpret these as meaningful global distances; the layout objective may not constrain them.
- **Cluster size and density:** plot area can be distorted. It does not directly report the original cluster size or density.
- **Axes:** the orientation and absolute coordinates have no direct meaning; rotating or reflecting a layout does not change its interpretation.
- **Stability:** results can depend on initialization, random seed, and settings. Compare multiple runs and parameter choices before treating a pattern as robust.

## Choosing a method

Use PCA for a fast, interpretable linear baseline and when a fitted transform for new data is useful. Use t-SNE to inspect local neighbourhoods, especially in smaller datasets; adding new points generally requires fitting the embedding again. UMAP can often transform new points after fitting and can scale to larger datasets, but the result still depends on its metric and neighbourhood settings. Neither method should be selected solely because its plot looks more separated.

These are primarily exploratory tools. Before using projected coordinates in a predictive model, validate the full projection-and-model pipeline on held-out data and check whether the representation generalizes to new observations.

## Common mistakes

- **Claiming clusters are proven by the plot:** separation may be an artifact of the embedding objective.
- **Reading exact distances or density from t-SNE:** those global quantities are not preserved.
- **Showing one run as definitive:** repeat with different seeds and reasonable settings.
- **Calling PCA invertible after reducing dimensions:** omitted components cannot be recovered exactly.
