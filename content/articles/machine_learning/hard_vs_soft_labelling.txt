title: Hard vs. Soft Labels
intro: Hard labels assign one target class to each example. Soft labels distribute target probability across classes, which can preserve annotator disagreement or communicate a teacher model’s uncertainty.

## What each label represents

For three classes, a hard label for class two is ([0,1,0]). A soft label such as ([0.1,0.7,0.2]) assigns a target share to each class; its values sum to 1. Hard labels are appropriate when examples have a clear agreed answer. Soft labels are useful when ambiguity or disagreement is part of the task.

## A worked example

Suppose five of seven clinicians mark a scan positive and two mark it negative. A hard majority vote records only “positive.” A soft target of ([2/7,5/7]) retains the disagreement. It does not prove the scan has a (5/7) chance of disease; it records how this group labeled it. The interpretation depends on how the targets were made.

Soft targets can also come from **label smoothing**, which moves a small amount of probability away from the one-hot class, or **knowledge distillation**, which trains a smaller model against a teacher’s class distribution. These methods can regularize training, but gains depend on the data and model and should be evaluated on held-out examples.

## What changes during training

With cross-entropy, a hard target penalizes probability assigned away from the labelled class. A soft target distributes that penalty across classes. This can discourage extreme confidence and preserve relationships among classes. It cannot correct an inaccurate target distribution, and soft labels do not automatically produce calibrated probabilities.

|Question|Hard labels|Soft labels|
|---|---|---|
|Target|One class|Distribution over classes|
|Best fit|Clear, agreed categories|Ambiguous cases, multiple annotators, or teacher outputs|
|Information kept|Chosen class|Relative target support across classes|
|Main risk|Throws away disagreement|Can encode noisy or poorly calibrated targets|

## When to choose each

Use hard labels when annotation is reliable and the task expects a single category. Keep annotator votes or confidence distributions when disagreement is meaningful. For label smoothing or distillation, compare against the hard-label baseline with the same data split and metrics; avoid tuning against the test set.
