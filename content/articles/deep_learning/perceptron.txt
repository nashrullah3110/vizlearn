title: Perceptron Classifier: A Practical Guide
intro: A perceptron is the smallest complete unit of a neural network: one weighted sum, one bias, one activation. Everything deeper is this repeated.

## The whole computation, in one line

A perceptron takes each input, multiplies it by a weight, adds them all up, adds a bias, and pushes the result through an activation function:

z = w<sub>1</sub>x<sub>1</sub> + w<sub>2</sub>x<sub>2</sub> + &hellip; + w<sub>n</sub>x<sub>n</sub> + b &nbsp;&nbsp;&rarr;&nbsp;&nbsp; output = f(z)

That is the entire model. The weights decide how much each input matters and in which direction; the bias decides how large the sum has to be before the neuron responds at all.

## Work one through by hand

Take two inputs with weights <span class="mono-font">w = [0.6, &minus;0.4]</span> and bias <span class="mono-font">b = 0.1</span>. Feed it <span class="mono-font">x = [1.0, 0.5]</span>:

z = (0.6 &times; 1.0) + (&minus;0.4 &times; 0.5) + 0.1 = 0.6 &minus; 0.2 + 0.1 = 0.5

With a <strong>Step</strong> activation the output is 1, because z is above zero. With <strong>Sigmoid</strong> the same z gives 1 / (1 + e<sup>&minus;0.5</sup>) = <strong>0.62</strong> &mdash; the same decision, but now with a sense of how confident it is. That difference is the whole reason step activations were abandoned.

## Try this above

1. Set <strong>Number of Inputs</strong> to 2 and pick <strong>Step (Binary)</strong>. The output can only ever be 0 or 1.
2. Switch to <strong>Sigmoid (Smooth)</strong> without changing anything else. Same weighted sum, but the output now slides continuously.
3. Switch to <strong>ReLU (Rectified)</strong> and make z negative. The output pins to exactly 0 and stays there.
4. Raise <strong>Number of Inputs</strong> to 5 and count the weights &mdash; one per input, plus a single bias regardless.

## Where a single perceptron fails

One perceptron draws exactly one straight boundary. That is enough for AND and OR, and famously not enough for XOR &mdash; no single line separates XOR's two classes, so no assignment of weights and bias will ever solve it. This limitation stalled neural network research for years; the answer was to stack perceptrons into layers.

The other trap is the Step activation itself. Its gradient is zero everywhere it is defined, so gradient descent has nothing to follow. You cannot train a step-activated network by backpropagation, which is why sigmoid and later ReLU took over.

## In one line

One weighted sum, one bias, one activation, one straight boundary &mdash; and a hard ceiling on what that can express.
