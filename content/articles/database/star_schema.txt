title: Star Schema: Fact Tables and Dimensions
intro: A star schema organizes analytical data around measurable events and the descriptive dimensions used to group them.

## Define the grain first
A fact table records events or measurements, such as one order line, one payment, or one daily inventory snapshot. Its grain is the precise meaning of one row. A dimension table describes entities used to filter and group facts, such as customer, product, store, or date.

A fact table can contain foreign keys to dimensions and numeric measures. Dimension tables are denormalized for convenient analysis. The layout resembles a star because dimensions connect to the central fact table.

## Why the grain prevents wrong totals
Suppose one fact row represents an order line. If a query joins it to a dimension with multiple matching rows, the line can be duplicated and the sum inflated. Before aggregating, verify that each fact key matches at most one dimension row for the relevant point in time.

Choose measures that can be aggregated as intended. Revenue can often be summed; a precomputed average generally cannot be summed or averaged correctly without its count or denominator. Ratios should usually be recomputed from additive components.

## Dimensions can change over time
A customer may move to a new region. A slowly changing dimension strategy determines whether historical facts use the old description, the new one, or both through versioned dimension rows. The choice must match the reporting question. A date dimension can also store calendar, fiscal, and holiday attributes that are awkward to derive repeatedly.

Star schemas are common in warehouses, but their details depend on the warehouse engine and modeling needs. See [OLTP vs OLAP](oltp_vs_olap_columnar.html) and [normalization](normalization_in_sql.html).

## State the grain before adding dimensions
Suppose one fact row is one product line on one order. Joining a line to a product dimension should match one product version for the order's relevant date. If the dimension contains multiple historical versions and the join omits the effective date, the line may match multiple rows and revenue is doubled. Add a test that counts matched dimension rows per fact key. When totals change after adding a dimension, inspect join cardinality before assuming the aggregation is wrong.

## Use conformed dimensions carefully
A shared date or customer dimension can make reports across fact tables consistent, but only if its keys and definitions mean the same thing in each model. Joining two fact tables through dimensions can still multiply rows when both have many records per dimension key. Aggregate each fact to a compatible grain before combining measures. Document which dimensions are shared and which attributes are historical. Consistent naming does not guarantee compatible business definitions; the warehouse model should make those definitions explicit.
