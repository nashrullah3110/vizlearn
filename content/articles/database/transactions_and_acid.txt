title: Transactions and ACID: A Practical Guide
intro: Four guarantees, wrapped around every BEGIN...COMMIT.

## What this is

A transfer between two accounts is two separate <a href="dml_in_sql.html" style="color: var(--accent-primary)">UPDATE</a> statements. If the database crashes between them, one account has lost money and the other never received it — unless the two are wrapped in a transaction, which guarantees they happen together or not at all.

## The four letters

- <strong>Atomicity</strong> — the whole transaction commits or none of it does. A failure midway rolls everything back, including steps that already ran cleanly.
- <strong>Consistency</strong> — a transaction moves the database from one valid state to another; it cannot leave money existing that came from nowhere.
- <strong>Isolation</strong> — concurrent transactions do not see each other's half-finished work, to a degree controlled by the isolation level.
- <strong>Durability</strong> — once COMMIT returns, the change survives a crash the instant after.

## Isolation levels, briefly

<strong>READ UNCOMMITTED</strong> lets one session see another's uncommitted changes — a "dirty read". <strong>READ COMMITTED</strong>, the default in most engines, blocks that: a session only ever sees data that has actually been committed. Stricter levels (REPEATABLE READ, SERIALIZABLE) exist to stop other classes of anomaly, at a cost in concurrency.

## Guided tour

1. <strong>Step through a clean transfer.</strong> BEGIN, debit Alice, credit Bob, COMMIT. Session B's balances only change at the very last step.
2. <strong>Switch to READ UNCOMMITTED and step again.</strong> Now B sees Alice's balance drop the instant A's UPDATE runs — before COMMIT. That is a dirty read, and it is why READ UNCOMMITTED is rarely the default.
3. <strong>Tick "Fail after the second UPDATE" and step through again.</strong> Both writes ran — Alice was debited, Bob was credited — but the transaction rolls back instead of committing, and Session A's own view snaps back to the original balances. Atomicity in one demonstration: a completed write is not a committed write.

## In one line

A transaction makes a group of writes behave as one unit: Atomicity guarantees all-or-nothing, Consistency guarantees the result is valid, Isolation controls what concurrent sessions can see of work in progress, and Durability guarantees a commit survives a crash. Isolation is a dial, not a switch — READ UNCOMMITTED trades correctness for speed by permitting dirty reads, which is why READ COMMITTED is the default almost everywhere.
