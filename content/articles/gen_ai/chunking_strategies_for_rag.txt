title: Chunking: A Practical Guide
intro: The retrieval step can only ever be as good as the pieces it is choosing between.

## Before the details

Every piece of <a href="rag.html" style="color: var(--accent-primary)">RAG</a> downstream of chunking — embedding, indexing, retrieval, reranking — operates on whatever the chunk boundaries produced. Cut a sentence in half and both halves lose the context that made the original sentence meaningful; the embedding of a fragment is not a fragment of the embedding.

## Fixed-size vs semantic

<strong>Fixed-size chunking</strong> counts a fixed number of words or tokens and cuts there, with no regard for what is at that position — a heading, mid-word, mid-sentence, anywhere. It is simple, predictable, and blind. <strong>Semantic chunking</strong> respects natural boundaries — sentences, paragraphs, headings — and only splits at those boundaries, accepting some variation in chunk size in exchange for every chunk being a coherent unit.

## Why overlap exists

Even a well-placed boundary loses something: whatever came just before a chunk starts is not in it, even though it might be exactly the context needed to make the chunk's first sentence make sense. Overlap re-includes the last few words of the previous chunk at the start of the next one, at the direct cost of storing and embedding the same words twice.

## Why documents have to be split

Three reasons, and they compound.

<strong>Embeddings blur when the input is long.</strong> A vector representing a 20-page document is an average of everything in it, so it is close to nothing in particular. Retrieval precision collapses.

<strong>The context window is finite,</strong> and shared with the question and the answer. Ten full documents will not fit.

<strong>Precision needs granularity.</strong> The user asked one question; the answer is one paragraph. Retrieving the whole document buries it.

So documents are split into chunks, each embedded separately. The chunk becomes the unit of retrieval, and how you choose its boundaries determines the ceiling on the whole system's quality.

## The strategies, from crude to careful

|Strategy|Splits on|Quality|
|---|---|---|
|Fixed size|Character or token count|Poor &mdash; cuts mid-sentence|
|Recursive|Paragraph, then sentence, then word|Good default|
|Structure-aware|Headings, sections, list items|Better for structured documents|
|Semantic|Where the topic shifts, measured by embedding distance|Best quality, highest cost|
|Sentence windows|One sentence, with neighbours as context|Good precision, needs expansion|
|Parent-child|Retrieve small, return large|Best of both|

<strong>Fixed-size</strong> splitting at 500 characters cuts sentences in half, separates a heading from its content and splits tables. It is the default in many tutorials and the first thing worth replacing.

<strong>Recursive</strong> splitting tries separators in order &mdash; paragraph break, then newline, then sentence end, then space &mdash; taking the largest unit that fits. It respects natural boundaries most of the time and costs nothing extra. This is the sensible default.

<strong>Structure-aware</strong> splitting uses the document's own markup: Markdown headings, HTML sections, PDF outline. A chunk is then a semantically coherent unit by construction, and the heading path can be prepended to give the chunk context ("Handbook &gt; Leave &gt; Parental leave").

<strong>Semantic</strong> chunking embeds each sentence and splits where consecutive sentences become dissimilar. It finds real topic boundaries and costs an embedding call per sentence at indexing time.

## Size and overlap

<strong>Size</strong> is a trade-off between context and precision:

|Chunk size|Effect|
|---|---|
|100 tokens|Precise retrieval, often missing context|
|200&ndash;500 tokens|The usual sweet spot|
|1,000+ tokens|Good context, blurred embeddings, fills the prompt|

<strong>Overlap</strong> of 10&ndash;20% means consecutive chunks share their boundary text, so a sentence spanning a split appears whole in at least one chunk. The cost is duplicated storage and occasionally two near-identical chunks in the results, which reranking or maximal marginal relevance handles.

The number that matters is not the token count in isolation but whether a chunk can <strong>answer a question on its own</strong>. A chunk beginning "This means that the limit is 30 days" is useless without knowing what "this" refers to &mdash; which is the argument for prepending the heading path, or for the parent-child pattern.

## Cutting documents up without cutting answers in half

Chunking decides what a retriever can ever return, and the default of a fixed character count fails in a specific way. This measures four strategies against the same document, and scores them on the only thing that matters -- whether a complete answer survives in one piece.

```python-run
import numpy as np

DOC = (
    "# Refund Policy\n"
    "Refunds are available within 30 days of purchase. "
    "To request one, email support with your order number.\n"
    "\n"
    "# Shipping\n"
    "Standard shipping takes 5 to 7 business days. "
    "Express shipping takes 2 days and costs an extra 12 dollars.\n"
    "\n"
    "# Warranty\n"
    "Hardware is covered for 24 months from the date of delivery. "
    "The warranty does not cover accidental damage.\n"
)

# the facts a user might ask about, each as the phrase that must survive
FACTS = {
    "refund window": "within 30 days of purchase",
    "how to refund": "email support with your order number",
    "express cost": "Express shipping takes 2 days and costs an extra 12 dollars",
    "warranty length": "covered for 24 months from the date of delivery",
    "warranty limit": "does not cover accidental damage",
}

print("A %d-CHARACTER DOCUMENT with three sections:" % len(DOC))
for line in DOC.strip().split("\n"):
    print("   %s" % (line if line else "(blank)"))
print()

def score(chunks, label):
    intact = []
    for name, phrase in FACTS.items():
        ok = any(phrase in c for c in chunks)
        intact.append(ok)
    return intact

def report(chunks, label):
    sizes = [len(c) for c in chunks]
    intact = score(chunks, label)
    print("   %s" % label)
    print("      %d chunks, sizes %s" % (len(chunks), str(sizes)))
    for i, c in enumerate(chunks):
        flat = c.replace("\n", " / ").strip()
        print("      [%d] %s%s" % (i, flat[:62], "..." if len(flat) > 62 else ""))
    for (name, phrase), ok in zip(FACTS.items(), intact):
        print("      %-18s %s" % (name, "intact" if ok else "SPLIT ACROSS CHUNKS"))
    print("      %d of %d facts survived in one piece."
          % (sum(intact), len(intact)))
    print()
    return sum(intact), len(chunks), float(np.mean(sizes))

results = {}

print("STRATEGY 1 -- FIXED SIZE. cut every N characters, no exceptions.")
N = 100
fixed = [DOC[i:i + N] for i in range(0, len(DOC), N)]
results["fixed, no overlap"] = report(fixed, "chunk_size=%d, overlap=0" % N)

print("STRATEGY 2 -- FIXED SIZE WITH OVERLAP. same cuts, but each chunk")
print("repeats the tail of the one before it:")
OV = 30
over = [DOC[i:i + N] for i in range(0, len(DOC), N - OV)]
over = [c for c in over if c.strip()]
results["fixed, overlap 30"] = report(over, "chunk_size=%d, overlap=%d" % (N, OV))
print("      overlap is a blunt instrument: it does not know where the")
print("      boundaries SHOULD be, it just makes sure every position")
print("      appears in some chunk with %d characters of context on each" % OV)
print("      side. it costs storage and retrieval slots in proportion.")
print()

print("STRATEGY 3 -- RECURSIVE. try to split on the biggest separator that")
print("keeps chunks under the limit, and only fall back to smaller ones:")
SEPS = ["\n\n", "\n", ". ", " "]

def recursive(text, limit, seps):
    if len(text) <= limit or not seps:
        return [text]
    sep, rest = seps[0], seps[1:]
    parts, out, buf = text.split(sep), [], ""
    for p in parts:
        candidate = (buf + sep + p) if buf else p
        if len(candidate) <= limit:
            buf = candidate
        else:
            if buf:
                out.append(buf)
            buf = p if len(p) <= limit else ""
            if not buf:
                out.extend(recursive(p, limit, rest))
    if buf:
        out.append(buf)
    return [c for c in out if c.strip()]

rec = recursive(DOC, N, SEPS)
results["recursive"] = report(rec, "recursive, limit=%d, separators %s"
                              % (N, [s.replace("\n", "\\n") for s in SEPS]))
print("      it tries paragraph breaks first, then lines, then sentences,")
print("      then words, so it only breaks mid-sentence when a single")
print("      sentence exceeds the limit -- and every fact survived.")
print("      but look at the chunk COUNT and the sizes. each section is")
print("      longer than the %d-character limit, so recursive splitting" % N)
print("      kept subdividing until everything fit, and produced %d small"
      % len(rec))
print("      pieces averaging %.0f characters. nothing is broken, and a lot"
      % float(np.mean([len(c) for c in rec])))
print("      of context has been scattered: the sentence about express")
print("      shipping no longer sits beside the one about standard")
print("      shipping. a limit set below your document's natural section")
print("      size turns recursive splitting into fixed-size splitting with")
print("      better manners.")
print()

print("STRATEGY 4 -- STRUCTURE AWARE. split on the document's own headings")
print("and ignore the character limit entirely:")
struct = [("#" + p).strip() for p in DOC.split("#") if p.strip()]
results["by heading"] = report(struct, "split on markdown headings")
print("      every chunk is now one complete topic. no fact is split,")
print("      because the document's author already decided where the")
print("      boundaries are and the splitter simply agreed with them.")
print()

print("THE COMPARISON:")
print("%-24s %10s %14s %16s"
      % ("strategy", "chunks", "mean size", "facts intact"))
for name, (intact, n, mean) in results.items():
    print("%-24s %10d %14.0f %13d/%d"
          % (name, n, mean, intact, len(FACTS)))
print()

print("WHAT THE NUMBERS SAY:")
print("   fixed-size chunking lost %d of the %d facts. it needs nothing"
      % (len(FACTS) - results["fixed, no overlap"][0], len(FACTS)))
print("   from the document and knows nothing about it, so its cuts land")
print("   wherever the arithmetic says. every fact it splits is a fact the")
print("   retriever can never return whole, however good the embeddings --")
print("   this is a ceiling imposed before retrieval is even attempted.")
print("   overlap recovered all of them, and that is genuinely what it is")
print("   for. it costs %d chunks instead of %d for the same document, so"
      % (results["fixed, overlap 30"][1], results["fixed, no overlap"][1]))
print("   storage, embedding cost and retrieval slots all rise, and the")
print("   duplicated text can return twice for one query.")
print("   recursive splitting also kept everything, but fragmented the")
print("   document into %d pieces because the limit was below the natural"
      % results["recursive"][1])
print("   section size. it is still the sensible default -- it respects")
print("   structure when there is any and degrades gracefully when there")
print("   is not -- as long as the limit is set with the document in mind.")
print("   structure-aware splitting produced %d clean chunks and lost"
      % results["by heading"][1])
print("   nothing, because the author had already marked the boundaries.")
print("   it is the best option when the structure is real -- markdown,")
print("   HTML, code -- and unavailable on a scanned PDF or a transcript,")
print("   which is most of the documents anyone actually has.")
print()

print("AND THE TENSION NOBODY ESCAPES -- chunk size trades two failures")
print("against each other:")
print("%-16s %-26s %s" % ("chunk size", "the risk", "what it feels like"))
for row in (("very small", "context is missing", "answers lack the 'why'"),
            ("small", "facts get separated", "retriever finds half"),
            ("large", "embedding is diluted", "relevant chunk ranks low"),
            ("very large", "the whole doc is one vector",
             "everything matches equally")):
    print("%-16s %-26s %s" % row)
print("   the dilution point is the one people miss. an embedding is a")
print("   single vector for the whole chunk, so a chunk covering three")
print("   topics has a vector that is near none of them:")
topics = {"refund": np.array([1.0, 0, 0]),
          "shipping": np.array([0, 1.0, 0]),
          "warranty": np.array([0, 0, 1.0])}
q = np.array([1.0, 0, 0])                  # a question about refunds
def cos(a, b):
    return float(a @ b / (np.linalg.norm(a) * np.linalg.norm(b)))
print("%-34s %16s" % ("chunk covering", "similarity to a refund question"))
for label, v in (("refund only", topics["refund"]),
                 ("refund + shipping",
                  topics["refund"] + topics["shipping"]),
                 ("all three topics", sum(topics.values()))):
    print("%-34s %16.4f" % (label, cos(q, v)))
print("   the same refund information, in a chunk that also covers two")
print("   other topics, scores %.4f instead of %.4f. it is still in there"
      % (cos(q, sum(topics.values())), cos(q, topics["refund"])))
print("   -- the retriever just will not rank it highly enough to return.")
print("   that is why 'put the whole document in one chunk' fails even")
print("   though it never splits a fact.")
```

## Experiments to try

1. <strong>Start at 30 words, fixed-size.</strong> Several chunks end mid-sentence — the cut counter is not zero.
2. <strong>Switch to semantic.</strong> The cut counter drops to zero at any size — chunks vary a little in length but every one ends on a real sentence boundary.
3. <strong>Shrink fixed-size chunks to 15 words.</strong> More chunks, and more of them are mid-sentence cuts — small fixed windows are the worst case for this failure.
4. <strong>Add overlap.</strong> The highlighted words at the start of each chunk (after the first) are the ones repeated from the end of the previous chunk — visible context carried across the boundary, at the cost of storing it twice.

## The short of it

Fixed-size chunking is simple and fast but blind to meaning, and will cut sentences in half whenever the count lands mid-sentence. Semantic chunking respects real boundaries at the cost of variable chunk sizes. Overlap recovers some of the context lost at any boundary by duplicating a small window of text between neighbouring chunks. None of this is free — every choice trades simplicity, storage, or coherence against the others, and the right trade depends on how structured the source documents already are.

## Parent-child, and sentence windows

Two patterns resolve the size trade-off rather than compromising on it.

<strong>Parent-child (small-to-big).</strong> Index small chunks for precise matching, but return their larger parent section to the model. Retrieval operates on 100-token units; generation sees the 800-token section they came from. You get the precision of small chunks and the context of large ones.

<strong>Sentence windows.</strong> Index each sentence individually, and at retrieval time expand to include the two or three sentences either side. Same idea, finer granularity.

Both require storing the relationship between the indexed unit and its context, which every serious vector store supports through metadata.

## What to store alongside the vector

Metadata is what makes a retrieval system usable in production, and it is easy to under-invest in at indexing time:

- <strong>Source</strong> &mdash; document id, title, URL. Needed for citations.
- <strong>Location</strong> &mdash; page number, section heading, character offsets.
- <strong>Heading path</strong> &mdash; prepend it to the chunk text as well as storing it.
- <strong>Dates</strong> &mdash; created and modified, so stale content can be filtered or down-weighted.
- <strong>Access control</strong> &mdash; the groups permitted to see it, filtered at query time.
- <strong>Document type</strong> &mdash; so a query about policy can be restricted to policy documents.

Adding metadata after the fact means re-indexing. Deciding it up front costs nothing.

## Document types that need special handling

<strong>Tables.</strong> Splitting a table mid-row destroys it. Extract tables separately, keep each whole, and store a text summary alongside for embedding.

<strong>Code.</strong> Split on function and class boundaries, not line counts. A half-function is not retrievable knowledge.

<strong>PDFs.</strong> Multi-column layouts are frequently extracted in the wrong reading order. Check the extracted text before indexing thousands of documents; a layout-aware extractor is worth the effort.

<strong>Transcripts.</strong> Split on speaker turns or topic shifts, and keep timestamps in the metadata.

<strong>Slides.</strong> One slide per chunk usually works, with the deck title prepended.

## Questions people ask

<strong>What chunk size should I use?</strong> Start at 300&ndash;500 tokens with 15% overlap and recursive splitting, then evaluate against your own question set.

<strong>Is semantic chunking worth the cost?</strong> Often yes for heterogeneous documents, and the gain over structure-aware chunking on well-structured documents is usually small.

<strong>Should chunks overlap?</strong> Yes, 10&ndash;20%. It is cheap insurance against boundary loss.

<strong>How do I keep context in small chunks?</strong> Prepend the heading path, or use parent-child retrieval.

<strong>Can chunk size differ within one index?</strong> Yes, and it is often sensible &mdash; a table or a code function is one chunk whatever its length.

<strong>How do I know my chunking is bad?</strong> Low recall@k on questions whose answers you know are in the corpus. Then read the retrieved chunks: fragments and mid-sentence cuts are the diagnosis.

## Recap in one screen

- Chunks are the unit of retrieval, so their boundaries set the ceiling on system quality.
- Recursive splitting on natural separators is the sensible default; structure-aware is better where structure exists.
- 200&ndash;500 tokens with 10&ndash;20% overlap covers most cases.
- The real test is whether a chunk can answer a question alone &mdash; hence heading paths and parent-child retrieval.
- Store source, location, dates and permissions at indexing time; adding them later means re-indexing.
