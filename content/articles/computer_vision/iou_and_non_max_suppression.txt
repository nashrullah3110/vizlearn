title: IoU and Non-Max Suppression: A Practical Guide
intro: Turning a pile of overlapping boxes into one box per object.

## The problem it solves

A detector's region proposal stage doesn't stop at one box per object — it scores hundreds of candidate boxes and keeps every one above a confidence floor, which for a single real object usually means a cluster of overlapping, near-duplicate boxes. <strong>Intersection over Union (IoU)</strong> is the standard way to measure how much two boxes overlap: the area they share, divided by the total area either one covers. <code>IoU = 1</code> means identical boxes; <code>IoU = 0</code> means no overlap at all.

## Non-Max Suppression

NMS turns that overlap score into a cleanup rule. Sort every proposal by confidence, descending. Take the top one, keep it, and discard every remaining box whose IoU with it exceeds a threshold — those are treated as duplicate detections of the same object. Move to the next surviving box by confidence and repeat, until nothing is left to process. The threshold is the only knob: too low and boxes on genuinely different but nearby objects get merged into one; too high and duplicate boxes on the same object all survive.

## Measuring overlap with one number

IoU compares two boxes by dividing the area they share by the area they cover together:

<p class="mono-font">IoU = intersection / union</p>

Worked through. Box A spans (0, 0) to (100, 100); box B spans (50, 50) to (150, 150).

- Intersection: from (50, 50) to (100, 100) &mdash; 50 &times; 50 = <strong>2,500</strong>.
- Union: 10,000 + 10,000 &minus; 2,500 = <strong>17,500</strong>.
- IoU = 2,500 / 17,500 = <strong>0.14</strong>.

Reading the scale:

|IoU|Interpretation|
|---|---|
|&gt; 0.9|Near-perfect box|
|0.7&ndash;0.9|Good localisation|
|0.5&ndash;0.7|Acceptable by the traditional threshold|
|&lt; 0.5|Usually counted as a miss|
|0|No overlap at all|

Two properties make it the standard measure. It is scale-invariant &mdash; a 20-pixel error on a small object scores the same as a proportionally similar error on a large one &mdash; and it is bounded, so it can be thresholded and averaged.

```python
def iou(a, b):
    x1, y1 = max(a[0], b[0]), max(a[1], b[1])
    x2, y2 = min(a[2], b[2]), min(a[3], b[3])
    inter = max(0, x2 - x1) * max(0, y2 - y1)
    area_a = (a[2] - a[0]) * (a[3] - a[1])
    area_b = (b[2] - b[0]) * (b[3] - b[1])
    return inter / (area_a + area_b - inter)
```

The two `max(0, ...)` calls are what handle boxes that do not overlap; without them a negative width times a negative height produces a positive area and a nonsensical result.

## Non-maximum suppression, step by step

A detector fires at many nearby positions, so one object produces a cluster of boxes. NMS reduces each cluster to one.

1. Discard every box below a confidence threshold.
2. Sort the rest by confidence, highest first.
3. Take the top box and keep it.
4. Remove every remaining box whose IoU with it exceeds the NMS threshold.
5. Repeat from step 3 with what is left.

Two thresholds control the outcome, and they do different jobs. The <strong>confidence threshold</strong> decides how sure the model must be before a box is considered at all &mdash; raise it for fewer false positives, lower it for better recall. The <strong>NMS IoU threshold</strong> decides how much overlap counts as a duplicate; 0.45&ndash;0.5 is typical.

The failure mode is genuinely overlapping objects: two people standing close together produce boxes with high IoU, and standard NMS deletes one of them. <strong>Soft-NMS</strong> addresses this by reducing the confidence of overlapping boxes rather than deleting them outright, so a strong second detection can still survive.

Note also that NMS is applied <em>per class</em> in most implementations &mdash; a dog box and a person box overlapping heavily are not duplicates of each other.

## Scoring box overlap, then removing the duplicates

Intersection over union is four max/min operations. Non-max suppression is a sort and a loop. Both are written out here, along with the two cases where each one behaves badly.

```python-run
import numpy as np

def iou(a, b):
    # boxes are (x1, y1, x2, y2)
    ix1, iy1 = max(a[0], b[0]), max(a[1], b[1])
    ix2, iy2 = min(a[2], b[2]), min(a[3], b[3])
    iw, ih = max(0.0, ix2 - ix1), max(0.0, iy2 - iy1)
    inter = iw * ih
    area_a = (a[2] - a[0]) * (a[3] - a[1])
    area_b = (b[2] - b[0]) * (b[3] - b[1])
    return inter / (area_a + area_b - inter)

A = (10.0, 10.0, 50.0, 50.0)
print("IOU, step by step. box A = %s, area %.0f" % (A, (A[2] - A[0]) * (A[3] - A[1])))
print()
print("%28s %10s %10s %10s %8s" % ("box B", "intersect", "union", "IoU", "verdict"))
for B, label in (((10, 10, 50, 50), "identical"),
                 ((20, 20, 60, 60), "shifted"),
                 ((30, 30, 70, 70), "corner touch"),
                 ((10, 10, 30, 30), "B inside A"),
                 ((60, 60, 90, 90), "no overlap")):
    B = tuple(float(v) for v in B)
    ix1, iy1 = max(A[0], B[0]), max(A[1], B[1])
    ix2, iy2 = min(A[2], B[2]), min(A[3], B[3])
    inter = max(0.0, ix2 - ix1) * max(0.0, iy2 - iy1)
    union = (A[2] - A[0]) * (A[3] - A[1]) + (B[2] - B[0]) * (B[3] - B[1]) - inter
    v = iou(A, B)
    print("%28s %10.0f %10.0f %10.4f %8s"
          % (str(tuple(int(x) for x in B)), inter, union, v,
             "match" if v >= 0.5 else "reject"))
print()
print("   the max/min pair is the whole trick: the intersection's left edge")
print("   is the RIGHTMOST of the two left edges, and its right edge is the")
print("   LEFTMOST of the two right edges. if that gives a negative width,")
print("   the boxes do not overlap and the clamp to 0 handles it.")
print()

print("WHAT 0.5 ACTUALLY LOOKS LIKE. two boxes of equal size, slid apart:")
print("%14s %10s %s" % ("offset", "IoU", ""))
for off in (0, 5, 10, 13, 20, 30, 40):
    B = (10.0 + off, 10.0, 50.0 + off, 50.0)
    v = iou(A, B)
    print("%14d %10.4f %s" % (off, v, "#" * int(v * 40)))
print("   a 40-pixel box shifted 13 pixels -- a third of its width -- still")
print("   scores about 0.5. the standard threshold is more forgiving than")
print("   it sounds, which is why COCO reports mAP averaged over 0.5 to")
print("   0.95 rather than at 0.5 alone.")
print()

boxes = np.array([
    [12, 12, 52, 52], [14, 15, 54, 55], [11, 9, 49, 47],   # three on object 1
    [80, 80, 130, 130], [82, 78, 128, 132],                # two on object 2
    [200, 40, 240, 80],                                    # one on object 3
    [36, 20, 76, 60],                                      # object 4, overlapping 1
], float)
scores = np.array([0.92, 0.88, 0.75, 0.95, 0.71, 0.66, 0.80])
names = ["A", "B", "C", "D", "E", "F", "G"]

print("NON-MAX SUPPRESSION. seven detections. objects 1, 2 and 3 are well")
print("separated; a fourth object G genuinely overlaps object 1:")
print("%6s %28s %8s" % ("box", "coords", "score"))
for n, b, s in zip(names, boxes, scores):
    print("%6s %28s %8.2f" % (n, str(tuple(int(x) for x in b)), s))
print()

def nms(boxes, scores, thresh):
    order = list(np.argsort(-scores))
    keep, log = [], []
    while order:
        i = order.pop(0)
        keep.append(i)
        survivors = []
        for j in order:
            v = iou(boxes[i], boxes[j])
            if v > thresh:
                log.append((names[i], names[j], v, "suppressed"))
            else:
                log.append((names[i], names[j], v, "kept"))
                survivors.append(j)
        order = survivors
    return keep, log

keep, log = nms(boxes, scores, 0.5)
print("   the algorithm: take the highest-scoring box, suppress everything")
print("   that overlaps it too much, repeat with what is left.")
print()
print("%10s %10s %10s %14s" % ("winner", "compared", "IoU", "outcome"))
for w, c, v, out in log:
    print("%10s %10s %10.4f %14s" % (w, c, v, out))
print()
print("   kept: %s" % ", ".join(names[i] for i in keep))
print("   IoU between A (object 1) and G (object 4): %.4f" % iou(boxes[0], boxes[6]))
print("   they are different objects that happen to overlap, and whether")
print("   NMS keeps both depends entirely on where the threshold sits.")
print()

print("THE THRESHOLD IS A REAL DIAL:")
print("%14s %30s %10s" % ("IoU threshold", "kept", "count"))
for t in (0.05, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.95):
    k, _ = nms(boxes, scores, t)
    print("%14.2f %30s %10d" % (t, ", ".join(names[i] for i in k), len(k)))
print("   at a low threshold G is deleted -- a real object lost because it")
print("   overlapped a more confident one. at a high threshold the")
print("   duplicates B, C and E survive. no single value is right for both.")
print()

print("WHERE IT FAILS. two people standing close together:")
crowd = np.array([[10, 10, 40, 70], [18, 10, 48, 70]], float)
crowd_scores = np.array([0.9, 0.85])
v = iou(crowd[0], crowd[1])
print("   two genuinely separate objects, IoU %.4f" % v)
for t in (0.3, 0.5, 0.7):
    k, _ = nms(crowd, crowd_scores, t)
    print("      threshold %.1f -> keeps %d box(es)%s"
          % (t, len(k), "  <- a real person deleted" if len(k) < 2 else ""))
print("   NMS cannot tell 'a duplicate of one object' from 'two objects")
print("   that overlap'. it only sees the IoU.")
print()
print("   SOFT-NMS is the usual repair: instead of deleting an overlapping")
print("   box, multiply its score down and let it compete again:")
for v_ in (0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9):
    print("      IoU %.1f -> score x %.3f (gaussian, sigma 0.5)"
          % (v_, np.exp(-v_ ** 2 / 0.5)))
print("   a heavily overlapping box is nearly removed; a marginal one keeps")
print("   most of its score and survives if it was confident enough.")
```

## Try it yourself

1. <strong>Read the stage at the default threshold.</strong> Box A (confidence 0.95) is kept. Box D, a separate object with no real overlap with A, is also kept. Boxes B and C overlap A too heavily and are suppressed as duplicates.
2. <strong>Lower the threshold toward 0.1.</strong> Nothing changes here — B and C already fail a much looser bar, so tightening it further has no effect on this particular layout.
3. <strong>Raise the threshold past roughly 0.59.</strong> Box B's overlap with A no longer exceeds the (now looser) suppression bar, so B is kept as a second detection of what is really the same object.
4. <strong>Raise it past roughly 0.81.</strong> Now C survives too — at this threshold NMS considers all four boxes different enough to keep, even though A, B and C clearly describe one dog.

## The short of it

IoU is a pure geometry calculation — it knows nothing about confidence or class. NMS is the policy layer that uses it: keep the most confident box, discard anything that overlaps it past a threshold, repeat. Set the threshold too low and it merges distinct nearby objects into one detection; set it too high and duplicate boxes on the same object all survive. Every object detector that outputs boxes runs some form of this after its raw proposals come out.

## IoU as a loss function

Since IoU is what evaluation measures, training directly on it is appealing &mdash; and it took several iterations to make it work.

Plain IoU has a fatal gradient problem: when two boxes do not overlap at all, IoU is 0 for every possible position, so there is no gradient telling the box which way to move. Three refinements fix it:

- <strong>GIoU</strong> adds a penalty based on the smallest box enclosing both, so non-overlapping boxes still receive a useful gradient.
- <strong>DIoU</strong> adds a term for the distance between the box centres, which converges faster.
- <strong>CIoU</strong> adds an aspect-ratio term as well, and is the default regression loss in several modern detectors.

The alternative, still widely used, is smooth L1 on the coordinates &mdash; simple and stable, but it optimises a proxy rather than the metric, and it treats a 10-pixel error the same on a small object as on a large one.

## Where IoU appears beyond detection

- <strong>Assigning anchors during training.</strong> An anchor box is treated as a positive example if its IoU with a ground-truth box exceeds a threshold, and as background below a lower one. Anchors in between are ignored.
- <strong>Segmentation.</strong> The same ratio computed over pixel masks rather than boxes is the Jaccard index, and mean IoU is the standard segmentation metric.
- <strong>Tracking.</strong> Frame-to-frame association is usually IoU-based &mdash; a detection is matched to the track whose last box it overlaps most.
- <strong>Evaluation at several thresholds.</strong> COCO's primary metric averages mAP over IoU thresholds from 0.5 to 0.95, which is what makes it reward tight boxes rather than approximate ones.

## Common mistakes

- <strong>Forgetting to clamp negative overlaps</strong>, giving a positive intersection for disjoint boxes.
- <strong>Mixing box formats.</strong> Applying corner-format code to centre-format boxes produces plausible-looking nonsense.
- <strong>Running NMS across classes</strong> when it should be per class, deleting legitimate detections of different objects.
- <strong>An NMS threshold that is too low</strong>, which removes genuine nearby objects; or too high, which leaves duplicates.
- <strong>Tuning the confidence threshold on the test set</strong> rather than on validation data.
- <strong>Assuming NMS is cheap.</strong> With tens of thousands of candidate boxes it can dominate inference time; batched GPU implementations exist for this reason.

## Questions people ask

<strong>What IoU threshold means "correct"?</strong> 0.5 by the Pascal VOC convention. COCO averages from 0.5 to 0.95 in steps of 0.05, which is stricter and now more standard.

<strong>Can IoU exceed 1?</strong> No &mdash; the intersection can never exceed the union.

<strong>Is NMS still needed?</strong> For most detectors, yes. DETR-style set-prediction models avoid it by design, which is one of their main attractions.

<strong>How do I handle heavily overlapping objects?</strong> Soft-NMS, a higher NMS threshold, or an architecture that predicts a set directly.

<strong>Should I train with an IoU-based loss?</strong> CIoU or DIoU generally beats smooth L1 on localisation quality, and both are readily available in modern detection libraries.

<strong>Why is my mAP low despite good-looking boxes?</strong> Often the strict IoU thresholds: boxes that look right to a human may score 0.6, which passes at 0.5 and fails at 0.75.

## Recap in one screen

- IoU is overlap divided by union &mdash; scale-invariant, bounded between 0 and 1.
- Clamp negative overlaps to zero, or disjoint boxes report a false positive area.
- NMS keeps the highest-confidence box and removes overlapping duplicates, per class.
- Two thresholds matter: confidence (how sure) and NMS IoU (how much overlap is a duplicate).
- GIoU, DIoU and CIoU make IoU usable as a training loss by supplying gradients when boxes do not overlap.
