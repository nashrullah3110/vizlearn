title: 1x1 Convolutions
intro: A kernel with no spatial extent. Why almost each modern architecture is full of them.

## The operation that looks like nothing

A 3&#215;3 convolution mixes a pixel with its eight neighbours. A 1&#215;1
convolution has no neighbours to combine. At first reading it appears to
times each pixel by a number. This Is a scaling and hardly worth a layer.

That reading forgets the channel dim, and the channel dim is where
all happens.

A feature map isn't a grid of numbers. It's a **stack** of grids, one per
channel. A layer with 256 channels holds 256 values at each spatial spot.
A 1&#215;1 convolution stands at one spot, takes all 256 values there, and
finds a weighted sum of them. Then does it again for each output
channel, and then repeats the whole thing at each spot with the same
weights.

So it doesn't mix neighbours. It mixes **channels**. Drag the two controls
above and watch the link pattern: each input channel reaches each
output channel, and nothing spatial happens at all.

## It is a fully-connected layer in disguise

Fix one spatial spot and the op is just a dense layer: `Cin`
inputs, `Cout` outputs, `Cin × Cout` weights. What makes it a convolution is
that the same dense layer is applied, unchanged, at each spot in the map.

That is the same weight-sharing arg that motivates convolution in the
first place. If mixing channels in a given way is handy at one location,
it's probably handy at all of them, and sharing the weights makes the layer
free of the input's spatial size.

## What it is actually for

**Changing the channel count.** This is the common case. A 1&#215;1 convolution
is the cheapest possible way to turn 256 channels into 64, or 64 into 256, and
it's why the layer is at times called a map.

**Bottlenecks.** Put a channel reduction before an costly spatial
convolution and an expansion after it. ResNet's bottleneck block is just
this: 1&#215;1 down to 64 channels, 3&#215;3 at 64, 1&#215;1 back up to 256.
The 3&#215;3 &mdash; by far the costliest part &mdash; runs on a quarter of the
channels, and the two 1&#215;1s cost almost nothing by check.

Count it. A 3&#215;3 straight from 256 to 256 channels needs
`9 × 256 × 256 ≈ 590,000` weights. The bottleneck version needs
`256×64 + 9×64×64 + 64×256 ≈ 70,000`. Same input and output shape, an eighth of
the params, and an extra two non-linearities thrown in.

**Adding non-linearity without touching detail.** Each 1&#215;1 is followed
by an activation, so a stack of them grows representational depth at
constant spatial size and negligible cost. This was the "network in network"
idea that named the technique.

**Replacing the classifier head.** Global mean pooling followed by a
1&#215;1 convolution does the job of a large dense layer with a fraction of the
params, and works for any input size.

## The arithmetic

The readout above checks the two counts right. For `Cin` input and `Cout`
output channels:

| Kernel | Weights | Sees |
|---|---|---|
| 1&#215;1 | Cin &#215; Cout | one position, all channels |
| 3&#215;3 | 9 &#215; Cin &#215; Cout | 3&#215;3 positions, all channels |
| Depthwise 3&#215;3 | 9 &#215; Cin | 3&#215;3 positions, one channel each |

That last row is worth noticing. A **depthwise separable** convolution splits
the work in two: a depthwise 3&#215;3 that mixes neighbours but not channels,
followed by a 1&#215;1 that mixes channels but not neighbours. Together they
rough a full 3&#215;3 at roughly a ninth of the cost. MobileNet is built
almost fully from that pair, and half of it's 1&#215;1 convolutions.

## The bottleneck, counted

The 1&times;1's main job is changing the channel count cheaply, and the ResNet
bottleneck shows why that is worth a layer. Compare a straight 3&times;3 from 256 to
256 channels against the bottleneck that squeezes down, does the spatial work
narrow, and expands back:

|design|weights|
|---|---|
|one 3&times;3, 256 &rarr; 256|9 &times; 256 &times; 256 = 589,824|
|1&times;1 to 64, 3&times;3 at 64, 1&times;1 to 256|256&middot;64 + 9&middot;64&middot;64 + 64&middot;256 = 69,632|

Same input and output shape, **8.5&times; fewer** params, and two extra
non-linearities thrown in &mdash; because the costly 3&times;3 now runs on a
quarter of the channels while the two 1&times;1s that resize the stack cost almost
nothing. That is the whole reason deep ResNets are affordable. The 1&times;1 is
doing no spatial work at all. Fix one spot and it's just a dense
layer, `Cin &times; Cout` weights, applied unchanged at each spot &mdash; so
it adds nothing to the [receptive field](receptive_field.html). Its entire
value is mixing channels. This Is just what the spatial kernels can't afford
to do at full width.

Beyond resizing channels, the 1&times;1 buys non-linearity at constant detail
&mdash; each is followed by an activation. So A stack of them deepens the network
spatially for almost nothing. This Was the "network in network" idea that named the
technique &mdash; and it's half of a depthwise-separable convolution, the pointwise
step that mixes channels after the depthwise step mixes neighbours. Two cautions: the
bottleneck is a genuine info bottleneck. So Squeezing 256 channels to 8 before
the spatial work can cost more fit than the work it saves. And a
1&times;1 with no activation after it, stacked on another linear layer, merges
into one linear map, because two mats times'd together are a single mat.

## Where it goes wrong

**Expecting spatial work from it.** It can't smooth, sharpen or find an edge.
If the receptive field needs to grow, a 1&#215;1 adds nothing &mdash;
its part to the [receptive field](receptive_field.html) is just
zero.

**Reducing channels too aggressively.** The bottleneck is a genuine info
bottleneck. Squeezing 256 channels to 8 before the spatial convolution saves
work and can cost more fit than it's worth.

**Losing the activation.** A 1&#215;1 with no non-linearity after it,
stacked on another linear layer, merges into a single linear map. Two
mats times'd together are one mat.

## A convolution that looks at one pixel

A 1x1 kernel has no spatial extent at all. This Sounds useless. It's a per-pixel linear layer across channels. It's what makes bottleneck blocks and depthwise-separable convolutions affordable.

```python-run
import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
H, W, C = 4, 4, 6
x = rng.normal(0, 1.0, (H, W, C))
print("a feature map: %dx%d positions, %d channels each." % (H, W, C))
print("   the vector at position (0,0): %s" % np.round(x[0, 0], 3))
print()
COUT = 3
W1 = rng.normal(0, 0.4, (C, COUT))
out = x @ W1
print("a 1x1 convolution with %d output channels is a %dx%d matrix applied"
      % (COUT, C, COUT))
print("to every position independently:")
print("   output at (0,0): %s" % np.round(out[0, 0], 3))
print("   by hand:         %s" % np.round(x[0, 0] @ W1, 3))
print("   shape %s -> %s" % (x.shape, out.shape))
print("   the spatial dimensions are untouched. only the channel count")
print("   changed, which is the whole purpose.")
print()
print("IT IS A DENSE LAYER, SHARED ACROSS POSITIONS. proof -- shuffle the")
print("positions and the outputs follow exactly:")
flat = x.reshape(-1, C)
perm = rng.permutation(H * W)
a = (flat @ W1)[perm]
b = flat[perm] @ W1
print("   identical: %s" % np.allclose(a, b))
print("   so it cannot mix information between positions. a 3x3 convolution")
print("   can; this cannot. it only mixes CHANNELS.")
```

The two main practical uses are cutting parameter counts and building bottleneck blocks.

```python-run
import numpy as np
print("USE 1 -- CHANGING THE CHANNEL COUNT CHEAPLY. compare the cost of")
print("reducing 256 channels to 64:")
for kh in (1, 3, 5):
    p = kh * kh * 256 * 64
    print("   %dx%d conv, 256->64 : %12s parameters" % (kh, kh, "{:,}".format(p)))
print("   the 1x1 does the same job for a ninth of a 3x3's cost, because")
print("   there is no spatial window to weight.")
print()
print("USE 2 -- THE BOTTLENECK BLOCK, which is what ResNet-50 is built from.")
print("a 3x3 convolution on 256 channels, done two ways:")
direct = 3 * 3 * 256 * 256
print("   direct 3x3, 256->256                    : %12s"
      % "{:,}".format(direct))
squeeze = 1 * 1 * 256 * 64
middle = 3 * 3 * 64 * 64
expand = 1 * 1 * 64 * 256
total = squeeze + middle + expand
print("   1x1 256->64  (squeeze)                  : %12s" % "{:,}".format(squeeze))
print("   3x3 64->64   (the actual spatial work)  : %12s" % "{:,}".format(middle))
print("   1x1 64->256  (expand)                   : %12s" % "{:,}".format(expand))
print("   %-40s: %12s" % ("bottleneck total", "{:,}".format(total)))
print("   %.1fx fewer parameters, and the expensive 3x3 now runs on 64"
      % (direct / total))
print("   channels instead of 256 -- a %.0fx saving on that layer alone."
      % ((3 * 3 * 256 * 256) / (3 * 3 * 64 * 64)))
print()
print("   and it is still non-linear: there is a ReLU after each of the")
print("   three, so the block is not equivalent to one big convolution.")
```

A 1x1 convolution also serves as a cross-channel mixer and turns a classifier into a fully convolutional segmenter.

```python-run
import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
C, COUT = 6, 3
W1 = rng.normal(0, 0.4, (C, COUT))
_ = rng.normal(0, 1.0, (4, 4, C))  # advance RNG to match earlier state
_ = rng.permutation(16)
print("USE 3 -- CROSS-CHANNEL MIXING. each output channel is a learned")
print("combination of all input channels. here is what one output channel")
print("is made of:")
for j in range(COUT):
    contrib = np.abs(W1[:, j])
    order = np.argsort(-contrib)
    print("   output channel %d draws most on input channels %s"
          % (j, ", ".join("%d (%.2f)" % (i, W1[i, j]) for i in order[:3])))
print("   a 3x3 convolution also mixes channels, but it spends 9 weights per")
print("   pair doing it. if the mixing is all you need, 8 of those 9 are")
print("   wasted.")
print()
print("USE 4 -- MAKING A NETWORK FULLY CONVOLUTIONAL. a dense layer needs a")
print("fixed input size; a 1x1 convolution does not:")
for size in (7, 14, 28):
    feat = rng.normal(size=(size, size, 512))
    logits = feat @ rng.normal(0, 0.1, (512, 10))
    print("   %2dx%-2d input -> logits %s -- one prediction PER POSITION"
          % (size, size, logits.shape))
print("   that is how a classifier becomes a segmenter: replace the final")
print("   dense layer with a 1x1 convolution and it outputs a class map")
print("   instead of a class, at whatever resolution you feed it.")
print()
print("the summary in one line: a 1x1 convolution is the cheapest way to")
print("change how many channels you have, and the only convolution that")
print("does nothing spatial at all.")
```
