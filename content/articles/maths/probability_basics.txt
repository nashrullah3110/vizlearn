title: Probability Basics
intro: Probability assigns numbers from 0 to 1 to events, with rules for combining uncertainty consistently.

## Events and their probabilities

A sample space lists possible outcomes; an event is a subset of those outcomes. For a fair six-sided die, the event “roll an even number” has three outcomes, so its probability is 3/6 = 1/2. The complement has probability P(Aᶜ) = 1 − P(A).

For any events A and B, the addition rule is P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B). The overlap must be subtracted because it is counted twice. If A and B are mutually exclusive, the overlap is empty and the simpler sum applies.

## Independence and repeated trials

A and B are independent when learning that one occurred does not change the probability of the other: P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B). Independence is an assumption about the process, not a consequence of events being different. For independent coin tosses, the probability of two heads is 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4.

Repeated independent trials with the same success probability lead to the binomial model; see [Bernoulli, binomial, and Poisson distributions](bernoulli_binomial_poisson.html). When information changes the probability, use [conditional probability](conditional_probability.html) and [Bayes’ theorem](bayes_theorem.html).

## Long-run frequency is not a guarantee

The law of large numbers says sample averages converge toward their expected value under suitable assumptions as the number of trials grows. It does not say short runs must balance out: a streak of tails does not make heads “due.”
