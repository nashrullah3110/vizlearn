title: Mean, Median and Mode
intro: Mean, median, and mode answer different versions of “what is typical,” so skew and outliers affect them differently.

## Three summaries

The mean is the sum divided by the number of observations. The median is the middle ordered value (or the midpoint of the two middle values). The mode is the most frequent value; a dataset can have multiple modes or none that is useful.

For 2, 3, 3, 4, 18, the mean is 6, the median is 3, and the mode is 3. The high value pulls the mean upward, while the median changes less. That makes the median more resistant to outliers, though it does not make it universally better.

## Choose for the question

The mean uses every value and is useful for many statistical models, especially with roughly symmetric data and limited extreme values. The median is often more representative for strongly skewed distributions, such as income. The mode works for categories as well as numbers and identifies a common value. See [quantiles and percentiles](quantiles_and_percentiles.html) for more robust summaries and [variance](mean_variance_standard_deviation.html) for spread around the mean.

A summary can hide structure: two very different distributions can share the same mean and median. Inspect the data or a plot when the distribution shape matters.

## Try this above

Add one increasingly large outlier and compare the movement of the mean and median. Then change a value’s frequency to see how the mode responds.
