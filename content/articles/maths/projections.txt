title: Projections
intro: A projection keeps the component of a vector that lies in a chosen subspace and discards the rest.

## Project onto a direction

For nonzero vector a, the projection of v onto a is the closest point to v on the line spanned by a. Its scalar coordinate along a is (a · v)/(a · a); multiplying by a gives the projected vector:

<div class="vz-math-block" role="math" aria-label="projection of v onto a equals a times a dot v divided by a dot a">projₐ(v) = ((a · v)/(a · a))a</div>

For a = (1, 2) and v = (3, 1), a · v = 5 and a · a = 5, so the projection is (1, 2). The residual v − projₐ(v) = (2, −1) is perpendicular to a: their dot product is zero. If a = 0, projection onto its direction is undefined.

## Project onto a subspace

With orthonormal basis columns in Q, projection onto their span is QQᵀv. The residual is perpendicular to every column of Q. Least-squares fitting uses this geometry: it projects observed data onto the column space of a design matrix. QR decomposition makes this calculation stable without explicitly forming the projection matrix; see [QR decomposition](qr_decomposition.html) and [orthogonality](basis_span_and_orthogonality.html).

A projection is idempotent: projecting twice gives the same result as once. It is not generally a rotation or a scaling; components outside the selected subspace are removed.

## Try this above

Move the vector and projection direction. Check that the residual stays perpendicular to the direction while the projected point moves along it.
