title: Conditional Probability
intro: Conditional probability recalculates an event’s chance within the smaller set where known evidence occurred.

## Restrict the sample space

If P(B) > 0, the probability of A given B is the fraction of B’s outcomes that also belong to A:

<div class="vz-math-block" role="math" aria-label="probability of A given B equals probability of A and B divided by probability of B">P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)</div>

In a standard deck, given that a drawn card is a face card, the chance it is a king is 4/12 = 1/3. The condition changes the denominator: we count only the 12 face cards, not all 52 cards.

Rearranging gives the multiplication rule P(A ∩ B) = P(A|B)P(B). Events are independent precisely when P(A|B) = P(A), provided the conditional probability is defined. Conditional probability is not generally symmetric: P(A|B) can differ from P(B|A).

## Why the direction matters

A medical test’s sensitivity P(positive|disease) is not the same as the chance of disease after a positive result P(disease|positive). The second depends on how common the disease is; [Bayes’ theorem](bayes_theorem.html) connects them. Confusing these conditionals is a common reasoning error.

For a sequence of events, the chain rule multiplies successive conditional probabilities. The events need not be independent; each factor accounts for what is already known.
