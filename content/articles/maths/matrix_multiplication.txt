title: Matrix Multiplication
intro: A grid of dot products &mdash; and the reason deep learning needs GPUs.

## The problem it solves

Multiplying two matrices produces a new matrix in which <strong>every single entry is a dot product</strong> &mdash; one row of the left matrix against one column of the right. Nothing more complicated is happening.

## The Rule for a Single Cell

Click any cell in <strong>C</strong> and the app highlights the blue row and amber column that feed it, then writes out the arithmetic term by term. Every cell is independent of every other &mdash; which is precisely why this operation parallelises so well on a GPU.

## The Shape Rule

You cannot multiply arbitrary matrices. The <strong>inner dimensions must match</strong>:

Drag the sliders and watch the shape line update. This single rule is behind most tensor-shape errors you will ever hit: a mismatch is not a subtle numerical problem, it is an operation that simply has no definition.

## Order Matters

Matrix multiplication is <strong>not commutative</strong>: <span class="mono-font">AB &ne; BA</span> in general. Often <span class="mono-font">BA</span> is not even a legal operation &mdash; a (2&times;3) times a (3&times;4) works, but (3&times;4) times (2&times;3) does not. When people say the order of layers matters, this is the literal reason.

It <em>is</em> associative, though: <span class="mono-font">(AB)C = A(BC)</span>. Choosing the cheaper grouping can save enormous amounts of computation, which is exactly the trick behind LoRA's low-rank update.

## Why This Is the Bottleneck

Multiplying two n&times;n matrices needs about <span class="mono-font">n&sup3;</span> multiply-add operations. Doubling the size makes it <strong>eight times</strong> more work. A single transformer layer with d = 4096 performs billions of these per token.

Watch the "Total ops" counter as you raise the sliders. That cubic growth is the entire reason GPUs &mdash; chips designed to do thousands of independent multiply-adds at once &mdash; became the hardware of deep learning.

## Guided tour

1. <strong>Click the top-left cell of C.</strong> Only the first row of A and first column of B light up &mdash; the rest of both matrices is irrelevant to that number.
2. <strong>Press "Walk every cell"</strong> to sweep through the whole output and see the pattern of row-column pairings.
3. <strong>Set n to 1.</strong> Each cell becomes a single product &mdash; this is the outer product, and it is exactly what LoRA's B&middot;A builds at rank 1.
4. <strong>Push all three sliders to 5</strong> and watch the operation count climb far faster than the matrices grow.

## Summing up

Row against column, multiply and add, repeat for every output cell. The inner dimensions must agree, the order cannot be swapped, and the cost grows cubically &mdash; three facts that explain tensor errors, layer ordering and the entire GPU industry.
