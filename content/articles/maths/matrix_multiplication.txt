title: Matrix Multiplication
intro: Each output entry is a row–column dot product; matching inner dimensions determine whether the product exists.

## The shape rule

If A has shape m × n and B has shape n × p, then AB has shape m × p. The shared inner dimension n must match. Each output entry is the dot product of one row of A and one column of B:

<div class="vz-math-block" role="math" aria-label="AB entry i j is the sum over k of A i k times B k j">(AB)ᵢⱼ = Σₖ AᵢₖBₖⱼ</div>

For example, [1 2] multiplied by the column [3; 4] gives 1·3 + 2·4 = 11. The result is one number because the output has shape 1 × 1.

## Products compose transformations

For column vectors, ABx means apply B first, then A. Matrix multiplication is associative, (AB)C = A(BC), but usually not commutative: AB and BA can have different shapes or values. The [matrix transformation lesson](matrix_as_transformation.html) shows this order geometrically.

The cost of multiplying m × n by n × p is proportional to mnp for the standard algorithm. For a chain of matrices, changing the grouping can therefore change the amount of work without changing the result. Deep-learning layers use these products to transform batches of feature vectors efficiently.

In code, NumPy uses `@` for matrix multiplication and `*` for elementwise multiplication. Check shapes explicitly when a product fails; do not transpose matrices just to silence an error without checking what operation is intended.

## Calculate a small product and check its shape

Let A=[[1,2],[3,4]] and B=[[2,0],[1,5]]. The upper-left entry of AB is the first row of A dotted with the first column of B: 1·2+2·1=4. The upper-right entry is 1·0+2·5=10. Repeating this row-column calculation gives AB=[[4,10],[10,20]]. The result is 2×2 because A's rows combine B's columns. If A is m×n and B is r×p, the product AB exists only when n=r, and its output is m×p.

Matrix multiplication is associative but usually not commutative: AB and BA can differ even when both exist. This reflects composition order: for column vectors, ABx applies B first. Elementwise multiplication is a different operation, often written A*B in array libraries; it requires compatible element shapes rather than matching inner dimensions. When an operation fails, inspect whether the intended calculation is a matrix product, transpose, or elementwise operation before changing shapes. For a chain of products, parenthesisation changes computational cost but not the result.

## Try this above

Associativity means a chain A(BC) and (AB)C gives the same mathematical result, but intermediate matrix sizes can differ dramatically. For example, multiplying a 100×2 matrix by a 2×1000 matrix costs much less than first multiplying the 2×1000 matrix by a 1000×1000 matrix in a larger chain. Choosing the grouping can reduce computation without changing the function. This is called matrix-chain ordering. In floating-point arithmetic, different groupings can also produce slightly different rounding, even though the exact algebra is associative.
