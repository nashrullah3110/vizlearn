title: The Chain Rule
intro: When one function depends on another, their rates of change multiply.

## Differentiate a composition

If y = f(g(x)), a small change in x first changes g, then changes f. The chain rule multiplies those two rates:

<div class="vz-math-block" role="math" aria-label="derivative of f of g of x equals f prime of g of x times g prime of x">d/dx f(g(x)) = f′(g(x))g′(x)</div>

For y = (3x + 1)², the outer function squares its input and the inner function is 3x + 1. Therefore y′ = 2(3x + 1)·3 = 6(3x + 1). At x = 1, the derivative is 24.

## Why backpropagation uses it

A neural network is a composition of functions. The chain rule propagates the loss derivative from the output through each layer to its parameters. Each local derivative contributes a factor; products that are consistently tiny or large can cause vanishing or exploding gradients. Activation choices, initialization, and normalization influence this behavior, but the chain rule itself is exact.

For several variables, the same idea uses Jacobian matrices: derivatives compose by matrix multiplication. See [partial derivatives and gradients](partial_derivatives_and_gradient.html) and [Jacobians and Hessians](jacobian_and_hessian.html).

## Try this above

Change the inner and outer rates separately. Notice that if either local derivative is zero, the derivative through the whole composition is zero at that point.
