title: Bayes’ Theorem
intro: Bayes’ theorem updates a prior probability by weighting it with how likely the evidence is under each explanation.

## Update a probability with evidence

For events A and B with P(B) > 0:

<div class="vz-math-block" role="math" aria-label="probability of A given B equals probability of B given A times probability of A divided by probability of B">P(A|B) = P(B|A)P(A) / P(B)</div>

The prior P(A) is updated by the likelihood P(B|A), then normalised by the total chance of the evidence. For competing explanations Aᵢ, the denominator is the sum ΣᵢP(B|Aᵢ)P(Aᵢ). The [conditional probability lesson](conditional_probability.html) explains why the direction of the condition matters.

## A medical-test example

Suppose a condition affects 1% of a population. A test has 90% sensitivity and 5% false-positive rate. Among 10,000 people, about 100 have the condition and 90 test positive; among the 9,900 without it, about 495 test positive. Thus a positive result corresponds to 90/(90+495) ≈ 15.4% chance of the condition, not 90%.

These values are illustrative; a real interpretation requires validated test performance for the relevant population. Changing prevalence changes the posterior even when sensitivity and specificity stay the same.

## Try this above

Adjust the prevalence while holding sensitivity and false-positive rate fixed. Notice how the posterior probability changes with the base rate.
