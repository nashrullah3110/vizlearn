title: The Normal Distribution
intro: The normal distribution is a symmetric, bell-shaped model determined by its mean and standard deviation.

## Shape and standardisation

A normal random variable X ~ N(μ, σ²) has centre μ and spread σ. Standardising gives Z = (X−μ)/σ, which follows the standard normal distribution when X is normal. A z-score counts standard deviations from the mean.

<div class="vz-math-block" role="math" aria-label="z equals x minus mu divided by sigma">z = (x−μ)/σ</div>

For a normal distribution, approximately 68%, 95%, and 99.7% of values lie within one, two, and three standard deviations of the mean. These are approximations, not exact coverage for every distribution. The normal model also has unbounded tails, which may be unrealistic for quantities such as proportions or physical measurements with hard limits.

## When the model is useful

Normal models describe many measurement errors and arise as approximations for sums or sample means under central-limit conditions. They are not justified simply because a dataset is large; check the data-generating process, skew, outliers, and dependence. See [the central limit theorem](central_limit_theorem.html), [sampling distributions](sampling_distributions.html), and [confidence intervals](confidence_intervals.html).
