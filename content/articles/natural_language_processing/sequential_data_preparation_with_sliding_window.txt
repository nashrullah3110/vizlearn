title: The Sliding Window: Turning One Sequence into Many Examples
intro: How raw sequential data becomes the (X, y) pairs that supervised learning requires.

## The Problem It Solves

Supervised learning needs pairs: an input <strong>X</strong> and the correct answer <strong>y</strong>. But a time series or a sentence arrives as one long, unlabeled stream. The sliding window converts that stream into training data by declaring: <em>"the last w values are the input, and the very next value is the target."</em>

## How It Works

Given a sequence of length N and a window of size w, slide the window one position at a time:

- Sample 1: X = [v&#8321;, ..., v&#8339;], y = v&#8339;&#8330;&#8321;
- Sample 2: X = [v&#8322;, ..., v&#8339;&#8330;&#8321;], y = v&#8339;&#8330;&#8322;
- ... and so on until the window hits the end, producing <strong>N &minus; w</strong> samples.

The same trick works for text: given the words "the cat sat on", predict "mat". That formulation — predict the next token from a fixed context — is the seed of the idea that grew into modern language models.

## Choosing the Window Size

- <strong>Too small:</strong> the model can't see far enough back — it misses weekly rhythms, long dependencies, sentence-level context.
- <strong>Too large:</strong> fewer training samples (N &minus; w shrinks), more parameters, and irrelevant ancient history dilutes the signal.
- <strong>Fixed forever:</strong> whatever w you pick, the network's view of the past is frozen at that width — a limitation that recurrent networks were invented to remove.

## Experiments to try

1. <strong>Click "Slide One Step" a few times.</strong> Watch the green window and amber target move together along the series, and each step append one row to the training table.
2. <strong>Press "Auto Play"</strong> and let the window consume the whole sequence. The sample counter stops at exactly N &minus; w.
3. <strong>Drag the window size to 6</strong> and replay. Bigger context per sample — but count how many fewer samples you end up with.

## In one line

The sliding window is the simplest bridge between sequential data and supervised learning: it manufactures labeled examples from an unlabeled stream. Its fixed width is both its strength (simplicity) and its weakness — the model can never look further back than w steps, which is exactly the limitation that motivates recurrent architectures.
