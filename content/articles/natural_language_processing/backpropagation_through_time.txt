title: Backpropagation Through Time, Step by Step
intro: How gradient descent trains a network that contains a loop.

## The Problem With a Loop

Backpropagation works on feed-forward graphs — signals flow one way, gradients flow back the same way. An RNN's feedback loop breaks that picture. The fix, <strong>Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT)</strong>, is beautifully blunt: <em>unroll</em> the loop into T copies of the cell, one per timestep, and the loop becomes an ordinary (deep) feed-forward chain that standard backprop can handle.

## Forward, Then Backward

- <strong>Forward pass:</strong> tokens enter left to right; each cell computes h&#8348; from x&#8348; and h&#8348;&#8331;&#8321;; the loss L is measured at the end.
- <strong>Backward pass:</strong> the gradient &part;L/&part;h starts at the loss and travels right to left. Crossing each cell multiplies it by the local derivative &part;h&#8348;/&part;h&#8348;&#8331;&#8321; — the chain rule, applied once per timestep.
- <strong>Weight update:</strong> because every unrolled copy shares the same W and U, their gradient contributions from all timesteps are <em>summed</em> before the single weight update.

## Exploration guide

1. <strong>Run the forward pass.</strong> Cells light up green in sequence, ending at the loss node — this is the network "reading" the sentence.
2. <strong>Backpropagate.</strong> Amber arrows sweep right to left. Watch the chain-rule product in the sidebar: 1.0, then 0.7, then 0.49... each timestep multiplies again.
3. <strong>Set T = 7 and repeat.</strong> By the time the gradient reaches t = 1 it is 0.7&#8310; &asymp; 0.12 — barely a whisper. The bar chart makes the decay unmistakable. This shrinkage is the seed of the vanishing gradient problem, covered next.

## Worth remembering

BPTT = unroll the loop, backpropagate through the chain, sum the shared-weight gradients. It makes RNNs trainable — but it also chains together T multiplications, and a long product of numbers below 1 races toward zero. That arithmetic inevitability is the vanishing gradient problem.
