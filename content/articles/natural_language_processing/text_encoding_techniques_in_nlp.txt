title: Four Ways to Turn Text into Numbers
intro: Label encoding, one-hot, bag-of-words, and TF-IDF — each fixes a weakness of the one before it.

## A Ladder of Encodings

Once you accept that text must become numbers, the question is <em>which</em> numbers. The four classic answers form a ladder — each rung keeps more information or removes more distortion than the one below it.

## Label Encoding & One-Hot

<strong>Label encoding</strong> assigns each vocabulary word an integer. It's maximally compact, but the integers imply a fake ordering — the model may conclude that word #7 is "more" than word #3. <strong>One-hot encoding</strong> removes that lie by giving each word its own dimension: a vector of zeros with a single 1. Honest, but the vectors are as wide as the vocabulary, almost entirely zeros, and every pair of words is exactly equidistant — no notion of similarity survives.

## Bag of Words & TF-IDF

<strong>Bag of words</strong> moves from single words to whole documents: each document becomes a vector of word counts. Suddenly documents can be compared — but frequent filler words like "the" dominate the counts while carrying no meaning. <strong>TF-IDF</strong> fixes that with a reweighting: term frequency (how often a word appears in <em>this</em> document) multiplied by inverse document frequency (how rare it is <em>across</em> documents). Words that appear everywhere score near zero; words distinctive to one document light up.

tf-idf(w, d) = tf(w, d) &times; log(N / df(w))

## Experiments to try

1. <strong>Walk the tabs left to right</strong> with the default corpus. Watch the representation grow from a simple ID list to a weighted document-term matrix.
2. <strong>On the TF-IDF tab, find "the".</strong> It appears in every document, so its score is 0.00 everywhere — the reweighting erased the noise word automatically. Compare with "chased", which only document 3 contains.
3. <strong>Add a new line</strong> like "the bird flew over the log" and rebuild. The vocabulary grows, every one-hot vector gets wider — a live demonstration of why vocabulary size is the scaling bottleneck for sparse encodings.

## What to remember

Sparse encodings are a progression of fixes: one-hot removes label encoding's fake ordering, bag-of-words adds document structure, TF-IDF suppresses noise words. What none of them can do is say that "cat" and "kitten" are related — for that you need <strong>embeddings</strong>, the subject of the next module.
