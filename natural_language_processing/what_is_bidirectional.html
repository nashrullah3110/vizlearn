Bidirectional RNNs: Reading Both Ways

Why understanding a word often requires the words that come after it.

The One-Way Blind Spot

Every recurrent model so far — RNN, LSTM, GRU — reads strictly left to right, so its state at position t summarises only words 1…t. But language does not resolve left to right: in “the bank of the river”, the evidence that disambiguates bank arrives after the word itself. A forward-only model must commit before it has seen the proof.

The Fix: Two Networks, One Sentence

A bidirectional RNN (Schuster & Paliwal, 1997) runs two completely independent recurrent networks over the same input:

  • The forward network reads token 1 → n; its state hₜ→ summarises the left context (everything before and including t).
  • The backward network reads token n → 1 with its own separate weights; its state hₜ← summarises the right context.
  • At each position the two states are concatenated: yₜ = [hₜ→ ; hₜ←]. Every position now sees the whole sentence — past on one side, future on the other. (Wrap the same idea around LSTM or GRU cells and you get the workhorse BiLSTM / BiGRU.)

Where It Shines — and Where It Cannot Be Used

Shines: any task where the full sequence is available before prediction — named-entity recognition, part-of-speech tagging, machine-translation encoders, speech recognition. Before transformers, BiLSTMs were the default encoder for virtually all of NLP.

Cannot be used: generation. When predicting the next word, the future does not exist yet — a language model must stay strictly forward (causal). This split survives today: BERT is bidirectional (an encoder that sees both sides), GPT is causal (a generator that must not peek).

Interactive Exploration Guide

  1. Run both passes and watch the green lane fill left-to-right while the amber lane fills right-to-left — two independent readers crossing in the middle.
  2. Click the first word. Its forward state has read almost nothing, but its backward state has read the entire sentence — edges are where one direction does all the work.
  3. Load the two “bank” presets and click bank in each. The forward context (“the”) is identical in both sentences — but the backward vectors differ sharply, because the right context (“river” vs “loan”) is what actually tells the two banks apart.

Key Takeaway

Bidirectionality is not a new cell — it is a wiring pattern: run any recurrent cell in both directions and concatenate the states. Each position then carries full-sentence context, which is exactly what understanding tasks need and generation tasks must forbid. Hold onto that forward/backward distinction — it returns as the encoder/decoder split in transformers.
